The Micro-Ecosystem of Our Orchards

Exploring pollinators, organic soil microbes, and the fragile biological ecosystem inside Himalayan orchards.

Dr. Kavita Sen

Soil Biologist & Sustainable Farming Advisor

Published

June 14, 2024

Core Focus

Organic Bio-fertilizers, Pollination

Companion Flora

Wild clover, Lavender, Local herbs

Our organic orchards are a self-sustaining biodiversity hotspot. Rather than utilizing synthetic chemical sprays, we manage the trees using companion planting and bio-fertilizers derived from fermented mountain nettle and cow manure.

High-altitude bumblebees and native wild honey bees (Apis cerana indica) are the silent caretakers of the fruit crops. They emerge in late spring to pollinate the blossoms, ensuring healthy, organic apples.

"An organic orchard is not a mono-cropped grid; it is a lively biological network where insects, soil microbes, and companion flora thrive together."

We also cultivate wild clover, lavender, and mountain herbs beneath the trees, which attract beneficial predatory insects like ladybirds, naturally controlling apple aphids and keeping the ecosystem in absolute balance.
