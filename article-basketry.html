Weaving History into Every Basket

Learn about high Himalayan wool and straw weaving history, and preserving local heritage craftsmanship.

Siddharth Negi

Director of Cultural Preservation

Published

September 24, 2024

Weave Origin

Sangla Valley, Kinnaur

Traditional Materials

Willow reeds, Wild Pine needles

Himalayan basketry is more than a craft; it is a physical codex of mountain survival. For centuries, rural villagers in Himachal Pradesh have woven dry willow reeds, pine needles, and wild hemp fibers into robust storage hampers (known locally as Kiltas) designed to haul firewood, fruits, and heavy loads across vertical slopes.

At the retreat, we partner with master elders from nearby Sangla valley to preserve these weaving guilds. Each basket represents approximately twelve hours of continuous hand-braiding, utilizing local harvesting methods that promote seasonal thinning of riverbanks.

"Each braiding step preserves a lineage of ecological wisdom, connecting local raw materials with practical agrarian utility."

By using these traditional baskets across our organic produce lines and homestay rooms, we support fair trade and introduce guests to the intricate geometry of native weaves.
