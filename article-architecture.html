Designing for Stillness: Architecture of the Peaks

How we integrated sustainable mountain architecture with modern luxury to create the DJ's Harvest retreat.

Deepika Chauhan

Farmer & Travel Enthusiast

Published

July 30, 2026

Architectural Style

Modern Kath-Kuni

Primary Elements

Local stone, Deodar timber, Glass

When designing the structures of the Himalayan retreat, our core philosophy was minimal intervention. The mountain slope dictates the foundation, not the other way around. We integrated local stone-cutting masonry, untreated pine timbers, and modern glass facets that frame the landscape like living art canvases.

We drew inspiration from Kath-Kuni architecture—a traditional building technique using alternating layers of dry stone and deodar wood without mortar, making structures highly flexible and naturally resistant to mountain tremors.

"Stillness is not just the absence of noise; it is a structural design logic where high ceilings, local timber, and insulation collaborate to filter the mind's clutter."

The interior spaces prioritize silence: high ceilings trap warm convective air currents, double-glazed panoramic windows provide superior acoustic isolation, and natural sheep wool rugs damp high-frequency echoes, creating a sensory sanctuary of pure peace.
